# Domain Generalization and Domain Adaptation Methods for Small-Sample Sensor/Acoustic Classification

## What are the current best-performing and most widely adopted domain generalization methods, and how have they performed specifically on sensor/audio/vibration data (not just vision benchmarks)?

### Takeaway
DANN (adversarial feature alignment), CORAL/Deep CORAL (second-order statistic alignment), MMD-based alignment (DAN/DDC), IRM, and meta-learning-based DG (MLDG and its fault-diagnosis variants) are all well established and have been *re-implemented and benchmarked on vibration/bearing fault-diagnosis data* (not just PACS/DomainNet/digits), consistently as cross-machine or cross-operating-condition transfer rather than cross-"recording rig" transfer in the sense SJWP needs; IRM in particular has serious, well-documented theoretical failure modes even in simple settings, which should lower confidence in it for a problem with only a handful of source domains.

### Cited Findings
- DANN (Domain-Adversarial Training of Neural Networks, Ganin et al., JMLR 2016, vol. 17, pp. 1-35) trains a feature extractor adversarially against a domain classifier via a gradient-reversal layer so features become discriminative for the task but domain-indiscriminate; trained end-to-end with standard SGD backprop — cited over 15,000 times, the de facto adversarial-alignment baseline. — [JMLR paper](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/volume17/15-239/15-239.pdf)
- Deep CORAL (Sun & Saenko, ECCV 2016 workshops) minimizes a differentiable "CORAL loss" = difference between source/target feature covariance (second-order statistics), computed batch-wise and added to the classification loss for end-to-end training; official PyTorch re-implementations exist publicly. — [Deep CORAL paper](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-319-49409-8_35); [PyTorch code](https://github.com/SSARCandy/DeepCORAL)
- In bearing-fault-diagnosis cross-domain studies, among marginal-distribution-alignment methods, DANN was reported to perform slightly better than CORAL, but both underperformed conditional-distribution-alignment methods — i.e., neither is the strongest available option once you can compare against newer conditional/subdomain alignment variants. — [Cross-Domain Open Set Fault Diagnosis, PMC9961812](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9961812/)
- `TL-Fault-Diagnosis-Library` (GitHub, Feaxure-fresh) is a maintained, public PyTorch library that implements DANN, CDAN, ACDANN, ADACL, DAN, CORAL, BSP, MDD (single-source DA); MFSAN, MSSA (multi-source DA); IWAN, AFN (partial DA); MCD; and for domain generalization: IRM, MixStyle, IBN-Net, MLDG, GroupDRO, VREx — all benchmarked on vibration/time-series bearing datasets (CWRU, MFPT, PU, XJTU, IMS, JNU), including cross-dataset (not just cross-condition-within-dataset) transfer. This is the single most relevant "applied to our modality" code/benchmark resource found. — [TL-Fault-Diagnosis-Library](https://github.com/Feaxure-fresh/TL-Fault-Diagnosis-Library)
- Meta-learning/episodic DG approaches have been directly built for bearing/rotating-machinery fault diagnosis: "Meta-GENE" (model-agnostic meta-learning with gradient-aligning algorithm for homogeneous/heterogeneous DG under unseen operating conditions); "MLDG"-style partitioning of multiple source domains into meta-train/meta-test to simulate distribution shift; "AdaMETA" (dynamic task-aware MAML + multi-scale attention, dynamic inner-loop learning-rate adjustment); an alternating meta-learning strategy combined with adversarial domain adaptation for task-agnostic, domain-invariant meta-knowledge. — [Meta-learning DG survey/papers roundup](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/iel7/9424/10355676/10091197.pdf); [Dynamic MAML cross-load bearing paper](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12562962/)
- Single-source domain generalization (only one labeled source domain, no multi-domain comparison signal) is recognized in the fault-diagnosis literature as a harder, separately-studied regime — e.g. "Multi-Scale Generative and Adversarial Metric Networks (MGAMN)" was designed explicitly because "collecting valuable fault samples from varying operational conditions is challenging" and often only one source operating condition is available. — [search summary citing MGAMN](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/331142664_Intelligent_Fault_Identification_Based_on_Multi-Source_Domain_Generalization_Towards_Actual_Diagnosis_Scenario)
- IRM (Invariant Risk Minimization) has a dedicated, influential critique: "The Risks of Invariant Risk Minimization" (Rosenfeld & Ravikumar, arXiv 2010.05761) proves that in the linear case IRM can recover the optimal invariant predictor only if the number of observed environments E exceeds the dimensionality of the environment-varying features (E > d_e), and shows a feasible non-invariant solution can achieve *lower* empirical risk than the true invariant predictor, so it is picked preferentially yet fails to generalize; a separate paper, "Does Invariant Risk Minimization Capture Invariance?" (Kamath et al., AISTATS 2021, arXiv 2101.01134), shows IRM's practical "linear" form fails to capture natural invariances even on toy problems modeled directly on IRM's own motivating examples. — [Risks of IRM](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2010.05761); [Does IRM Capture Invariance?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.01134)
- A paper specifically addressing synthetic-to-real transfer for fault diagnosis exists: "Numerical Simulation Data-Aided Domain-Adaptive Generalization Method for Fault Diagnosis" (PMC12158271) — directly analogous to SJWP's EPANET/synthesizer-to-real-Mendeley-recordings setup — but full-text access via WebFetch failed (PMC redirect) and could not be read in detail; only the title/topic was confirmed via search. This is a high-priority paper for a human to pull and read directly. — [PMC12158271](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12158271/)

### Inferences
- None of the sources found demonstrate DANN/CORAL/MMD/IRM/meta-DG on acoustic *leak* detection or on genuinely independent recording rigs in the sense SJWP needs (train on synthetic + a couple of real sites, test on an unseen real pipe network); the closest matches are bearing-vibration cross-machine/cross-dataset transfer, which is the same sensor modality family (accelerometer/vibration, short time windows) but a different failure mode (mechanical fault signatures, not leak acoustics) and typically richer labeled data per source domain than SJWP has.
- Because IRM has documented failure to recover invariance with few environments, and SJWP would have at most a handful of true "environments" (EPANET-synthetic + 2-3 Mendeley recording setups), IRM should be treated with low confidence relative to DANN/CORAL/MMD, which at least have direct empirical validation on vibration-sensor cross-dataset transfer via the TL-Fault-Diagnosis-Library benchmarks.
- The TL-Fault-Diagnosis-Library is a strong practical resource: it is public, implements most of the methods requested, and is pre-built for exactly the modality (1D vibration/time-series) and evaluation style (cross-dataset) closest to SJWP's problem; adapting its DANN/CORAL/DAN implementations to acoustic leak windows is more defensible than writing new code from the image-benchmark DG literature.

### Gaps
- Could not confirm, from the sources gathered, any DG/DA evaluation that uses explicit bootstrap confidence intervals over recordings (as SJWP's own protocol requires) — the fault-diagnosis literature overwhelmingly reports point-accuracy across conditions.
- Could not obtain full text of the synthetic-to-real fault diagnosis paper (PMC12158271); its architecture, sample sizes, and whether source domains are class-balanced remain unconfirmed.
- No source found directly reports DG/DA method performance on leak/no-leak acoustic classification specifically (as opposed to rotating-machinery fault classes); this is a genuine gap in the literature as searched, not just an omission in these notes.

## What unsupervised/source-free domain adaptation and test-time adaptation methods (entropy minimization/Tent, pseudo-labeling, teacher-student consistency, BN-statistics adaptation) have been applied to acoustic, vibration, or other 1D time-series sensor classification, especially where the target domain has zero or very few labels?

### Takeaway
Tent (entropy minimization via BN affine-parameter updates) and SHOT (source-free DA via information maximization + pseudo-labeling) are both well-cited, code-available methods; both have been applied to acoustic/audio classification under domain shift in at least one recent empirical study, and classifier-ensemble-based source-free DA has been specifically proposed for time-series classification, but none of the acoustic applications found use genuinely cross-recording-rig/cross-site audio in the way SJWP's Mendeley-testbed evaluation does — most use synthetic noise injection or digit/vision domain shifts as the testbed.

### Cited Findings
- Tent ("Tent: Fully Test-Time Adaptation by Entropy Minimization," Wang, Shelhamer, Liu, Olshausen, Darrell; ICLR 2021) adapts a model using only test data and its own parameters by minimizing the Shannon entropy of predictions over each test batch, updating only the channel-wise affine parameters of batch-normalization layers (not the whole network) and re-estimating BN normalization statistics online. Validated on corrupted ImageNet/CIFAR-10/100 (new SOTA on ImageNet-C), and on source-free DA benchmarks: digit recognition SVHN→MNIST/MNIST-M/USPS, GTA→Cityscapes segmentation, and VisDA-C — i.e., validated **only on vision benchmarks** in the original paper. — [Tent, arXiv 2006.10726](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.10726); [ICLR OpenReview](https://openreview.net/pdf?id=uXl3bZLkr3c)
- SHOT ("Do We Really Need to Access the Source Data? Source Hypothesis Transfer for Unsupervised Domain Adaptation," Liang, Hu, Feng; ICML 2020, arXiv 2002.08546) freezes the source-trained classifier head and fine-tunes only the feature extractor on unlabeled target data, using (a) an information-maximization loss that pushes target predictions toward confident, diverse one-hot outputs and (b) self-supervised pseudo-labeling (via weighted k-means-style clustering) for an added cross-entropy term — the source data itself is never needed after pretraining (fully source-free). Code is public. — [SHOT paper](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v119/liang20a/liang20a.pdf); [SHOT code](https://github.com/tim-learn/SHOT)
- "An Investigation of Test-time Adaptation for Audio Classification under Background Noise" (arXiv 2507.15523, 2025) is described by its own authors as "the first study to leverage TTA techniques for audio classification under domain shift." It evaluates TTT, Tent, and CoNMix (plus the authors' own modified CoNMix) on AudioMNIST and SpeechCommands V1 under injected background noise at varying SNR; the modified CoNMix reached 5.31% error at 10 dB (exercise-bike noise) and 12.75% error at 3 dB (running-tap noise) on AudioMNIST, beating the other TTA baselines in the reported settings. Full architecture detail, exact per-domain sample counts, and code availability were not recoverable from the fetched excerpt — only the abstract-level summary was accessible. — [arXiv 2507.15523](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.15523)
- "Classifier ensemble based source-free domain adaptation for time series classification" (ScienceDirect, 2025) is explicitly aimed at the SJWP-relevant modality (1D time series, source-free, i.e., no source data needed at adaptation time) but full text (method, datasets, sample sizes) could not be retrieved — only the title/topic was found via search; this is a priority paper for direct follow-up. — [ScienceDirect listing](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0950705125016235)
- Earlier adversarial/statistics-matching UDA for acoustic scene classification (not leak detection, but same sensor-modality family — microphone recordings, device/domain mismatch) has real precedent: "Unsupervised adversarial domain adaptation for acoustic scene classification" (arXiv 1808.05777, 2018) used the DCASE 2018 Challenge Task 1 Subtask B dataset (recordings from multiple real devices with mismatched recording conditions), trained a pre-trained classifier then adapted it adversarially using **unlabeled** target-device data, and reported roughly a 10% accuracy improvement on unlabeled target-device data while preserving source-domain accuracy. This paper does use genuinely different, real recording devices as its domains (not synthetic shift), the closest analogue found to SJWP's cross-rig evaluation. — [arXiv 1808.05777](https://arxiv.org/abs/1808.05777)
- A related method, "Unsupervised Domain Adaptation for Acoustic Scene Classification Using Band-Wise Statistics Matching" (2020) — a BN-statistics-style adaptation approach — matches per-frequency-band statistics between source and target device recordings (an audio analogue of AdaBN) rather than using an adversarial loss; it is unsupervised (no target labels) and was evaluated on both device-dependent and device-independent adaptation scenarios, with associated data on Zenodo. Exact recordings-per-domain counts and compute cost could not be extracted from the fetched content. — [arXiv 2005.00145](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2005.00145); [Zenodo data](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.1401995)

### Inferences
- The acoustic-scene-classification UDA literature (DCASE-based) is the best available proxy for SJWP's "different real recording rigs, unlabeled target" problem, because it already uses genuinely different physical microphones/devices as domains rather than synthetic corruption — but it is scene classification (10 urban environment classes), not binary anomaly/leak detection, and typically has far more labeled recordings per source domain (DCASE subsets run into the thousands of clips) than SJWP's handful of Mendeley recordings per site.
- Tent and SHOT are both lightweight enough (Tent updates only BN affine parameters; SHOT fine-tunes a frozen-head feature extractor) to plausibly run with SJWP's compute budget (single GPU) and even fairly small target batches, since both operate per-batch/online rather than requiring full retraining — but neither's published validation includes small-N, low-label 1D sensor data, so their published entropy/pseudo-label statistics (confidence thresholds, batch-size requirements) cannot be assumed to transfer without empirical check on SJWP's own data.
- Given Tent and SHOT both rely on the *target* batch having a reasonably balanced/representative mix of classes within each adaptation batch (entropy minimization and pseudo-label clustering both implicitly assume the batch spans the decision boundary), and SJWP's real Mendeley test data is heavily class-imbalanced (loop-only clean test set is real but the overall real dataset is reported as ~80% leak, per AGENTS.md), both methods are at meaningful risk of degenerate collapse (e.g., entropy-minimizing toward the majority class) if applied naively to small, imbalanced target batches — this needs explicit testing, not an assumption of transfer.

### Gaps
- No source found evaluates Tent, SHOT, or any source-free/TTA method on leak-detection, pipe acoustic, or water-infrastructure sensor data specifically.
- Could not determine from available excerpts whether CoNMix, SHOT, or Tent have published minimum-batch-size or minimum-labeled-target-count requirements; this matters directly for SJWP's "dozens to low hundreds of labeled examples" compute/data constraint and needs follow-up (ideally reading the SHOT and CoNMix papers in full, not just abstracts).
- Could not retrieve full text of the classifier-ensemble source-free time-series DA paper (ScienceDirect, paywalled) — architecture, dataset, and whether it assumes balanced per-domain labels are unconfirmed.

## For each method, assess applicability when source domains are small (tens to low hundreds of recordings each) and confounded (every site is single-class leak OR no-leak, never both) — where does the balanced-source-label assumption break?

### Takeaway
Essentially every DG/DA method found (DANN, CORAL, MMD/DAN, IRM, meta-learning DG, SHOT, Tent) was designed and validated assuming each source/target domain contains a reasonable mix of both classes; SJWP's actual data-generating structure — each real site is single-class (all-leak or all-no-leak) — violates this assumption for essentially all of them in a way that is not addressed in the literature surveyed, and this is the single most important critical-assessment finding of this research pass.

### Cited Findings
- DANN's domain classifier and CORAL/MMD's distribution-alignment losses are computed per-domain over a batch that is implicitly assumed to contain the task-relevant class distribution; the TL-Fault-Diagnosis-Library benchmarks (CWRU, MFPT, PU, XJTU, IMS, JNU) all structure each "domain" as a different operating condition or machine **with multiple fault classes present in each domain**, not a domain that is single-class by construction. — [TL-Fault-Diagnosis-Library](https://github.com/Feaxure-fresh/TL-Fault-Diagnosis-Library)
- The fault-diagnosis DG literature does separately recognize class-imbalance-within-domain as a problem (e.g., "A domain generalization network for imbalanced machinery fault diagnosis," Nature Sci Rep 2024, s41598-024-75088-8 — full text was not accessible due to a login/paywall redirect during this research pass), but the framing found via search describes *imbalance* (normal class over-represented vs. fault class) within each domain, not the more extreme SJWP case of a domain containing **only one class with zero examples of the other** — this is a stronger violation than anything explicitly modeled in the papers surveyed. — [Nature paper, access blocked](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-024-75088-8)
- "One-Class Domain Adaptation via Meta-Learning" and related single-class/open-set DG works found in search address a related but distinct problem: generalizing from a normal-only (one-class) training domain to detect multiple unseen anomaly classes at test time in a *single* domain's shift, not the SJWP structure of multiple small source domains each single-class but with the two classes split across different sites. — [arXiv 2501.13052](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.13052v1)
- Semi-supervised domain generalization work ("Overcoming Data Inequality across Domains," arXiv 2403.05209) addresses the case where only one domain is *labeled* and the rest are unlabeled — a different inequality (label availability) than SJWP's case (label availability is fine, but label *diversity within a domain* is zero), so it does not directly transfer as a solution either. — [arXiv 2403.05209](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.05209)

### Inferences
- Because DANN/CORAL/MMD-style alignment losses operate on marginal or class-conditional feature distributions per domain, and SJWP's domains are single-class, these methods can at best align the *marginal* feature distribution of (e.g.) "no-leak, Mendeley Looped" against "mixed leak/no-leak, synthetic" — there is no way to compute a class-conditional (leak vs. no-leak) alignment term using only single-class real domains, so any method relying on conditional alignment (explicitly flagged in the fault-diagnosis literature as outperforming marginal-only DANN/CORAL) is not usable as published without modification.
- A more defensible strategy implied by the literature, though not found stated explicitly in any single source, is to treat the synthetic (balanced, multi-class) domain as the only source for class-conditional alignment/classifier training, and use the single-class real domains purely as *unlabeled* target data for marginal alignment (CORAL/MMD on global feature statistics) or for BN-statistics adaptation (AdaBN-style, as in the band-wise-statistics-matching acoustic scene paper) — since those only require matching first/second-order statistics, not class-balanced batches. This inference is not directly sourced and should be treated as a design hypothesis, not a validated finding.
- SHOT's pseudo-labeling and Tent's entropy minimization are both actively dangerous in a single-class target domain: if a target domain is **all no-leak**, confident pseudo-labels / low entropy predictions that happen to be wrong (confidently predicting "leak") would be reinforced with no counter-signal, since there are no true leak examples in that domain to correct the drift. This is a direct, sourced mechanical property of both methods (entropy minimization over a batch; pseudo-label self-training) applied to the described data structure, not a hypothetical — it follows from how the published methods are defined. — [Tent, arXiv 2006.10726](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.10726); [SHOT, arXiv 2002.08546](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2002.08546)

### Gaps
- No source found explicitly studies or benchmarks DG/DA methods under the exact confound SJWP has (every source domain single-class). This appears to be a genuine, unaddressed gap in the surveyed DG/DA literature, not a search failure — searches for "single-class domain," "one class per source," and "imbalanced domain generalization" returned either (a) one-class-vs-multi-class open-set problems, (b) within-domain class imbalance (not full single-class), or (c) label-availability inequality across domains — none matching SJWP's structure precisely.
- Could not determine whether any published method explicitly separates "class-conditional alignment" (needs balanced domains) from "marginal/statistics-only alignment" (works with single-class domains) as a selectable mode; this would need to be verified by reading full method implementations (e.g., in TL-Fault-Diagnosis-Library's code) rather than abstracts.
- Did not find any paper discussing failure modes of SHOT/Tent/pseudo-labeling specifically under single-class target domains; the risk described above is an inference from the methods' mechanics, not a cited empirical finding, and should be flagged as such to the report writer.
