# Self-Supervised Representation Learning and Pretrained Audio Foundation Models for Industrial Acoustic/Vibration Transfer

## What contrastive / masked-spectrogram / wav2vec-style methods exist, and have any been evaluated on industrial acoustic/vibration anomaly detection, fault diagnosis, or pipeline/structural monitoring specifically (not just speech/music)?

### Takeaway
The named architectures (SSAST, AudioMAE, BEATs, wav2vec 2.0, HuBERT, data2vec, COLA, CLAR) were developed and benchmarked almost entirely on speech/music/general-AudioSet tasks; direct, named evaluation of these exact architectures on industrial acoustic/vibration anomaly detection is thin. The closest real evidence is (a) DCASE anomalous-sound-detection (ASD) papers that adapt generic "self-supervised pre-trained models trained on large-scale speech and audio data" (unspecified which architectures) with LoRA/adapters, and (b) a separate, more concrete line of work pretraining masked-autoencoder/contrastive transformers directly on vibration/sensor time series (not repurposed speech SSL models) for bearing fault diagnosis and structural health monitoring (SHM).

### Cited Findings
- COLA (Contrastive Learning of General-Purpose Audio Representations) pretrains on AudioSet with a same-clip-vs-different-clip contrastive objective and transfers to 9 downstream classification tasks spanning speech, music, acoustic scenes, and animal sounds, outperforming the earlier TRILL embeddings — but none of COLA's own evaluated downstream tasks are industrial/vibration — [arXiv:2010.10915](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2010.10915), [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2010.10915).
- A paper titled "Exploring Self-Supervised Audio Models for Generalized Anomalous Sound Detection" (arXiv:2508.12230) fine-tunes self-supervised pretrained models from large-scale speech/audio data on the DCASE 2020–2024 ASD challenge benchmarks, using LoRA fine-tuning plus a "Machine-aware Group Adapter" and a vector-quantization + dual-level contrastive loss to cluster unattributed data; it reports "significant improvements" across the five DCASE ASD challenge years, but the abstract does not name the specific SSL backbone(s) used (e.g., does not confirm BEATs/wav2vec/HuBERT by name) and gives no concrete AUC numbers in the abstract text we could retrieve — [arXiv:2508.12230](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.12230).
- A related paper, "Improving Anomalous Sound Detection via Low-Rank Adaptation Fine-Tuning of Pre-Trained Audio Models" (arXiv:2409.07016), exists and is specifically about LoRA-fine-tuning pretrained audio models for ASD, but its PDF could not be parsed by our fetch tool (returned corrupted/binary content), so we could not confirm which backbone models, datasets, or quantitative results it reports — flagged as a gap below rather than guessed — [arXiv:2409.07016](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.07016).
- "Anomalous Sound Detection using Audio Representation with Machine ID based Contrastive Learning Pretraining" (arXiv:2304.03588) uses a contrastive pretraining scheme that incorporates machine-ID labels and a self-supervised ID classifier; the search-result summary states it "outperforms state-of-the-art techniques using contrastive learning or self-supervised classification" and gains "approximately 6% AUC" improvement on DCASE-style benchmarks, but our direct PDF fetch failed (binary/corrupted content), so the exact backbone and dataset-split details are unconfirmed from primary text — [arXiv:2304.03588](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.03588).
- Outside of repurposed speech-SSL, there is a separate body of work pretraining transformer/MAE-style models directly on numerical vibration/sensor time series (not spectrograms derived from speech-domain SSL) for industrial fault diagnosis: FreqCondNorm pretrains a Transformer with masked autoencoding + contrastive learning with balanced domain sampling across five public predictive-maintenance datasets (CWRU, MFPT, UOC18, PRONOSTIA, CMAPSS) and reports 99.2% fault-diagnosis accuracy on CWRU (+6.4 points over a CNN baseline) — [arXiv:2609.20535](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20535).
- "Foundation Models for Structural Health Monitoring" (arXiv:2404.02944) is the first paper to propose Transformer Masked-Auto-Encoder architectures explicitly as "Foundation Models for SHM," pretrained via self-supervision on vibration data from bridges/viaducts, then fine-tuned for anomaly detection and traffic-load estimation, reportedly outperforming traditional methods — [arXiv:2404.02944](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.02944).
- A DAS (Distributed Acoustic Sensing) foundation model, "MAEPD," uses a Masked Autoencoder framework pretrained on large collections of 2D DAS signal data for signal recognition — this is structural/geophysical acoustic sensing, not speech-SSL repurposing, but is the same MAE-pretraining principle applied to a real sensing modality — [arXiv:2508.04316](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.04316).
- No evidence was found in our searches of CLAR (Contrastive Learning for Auditory Representations) being applied to any industrial/vibration task; all retrievable material concerns its original speech-emotion/general-audio benchmarks (not independently verified in this pass — flagged as gap).

### Inferences
- There appear to be two largely separate research threads: (1) "take a speech/AudioSet-pretrained SSL model and fine-tune/adapt it (LoRA, adapters) for DCASE-style anomalous sound detection," and (2) "pretrain a transformer/MAE directly on the target sensing modality's own numeric time series (vibration, DAS) using masked reconstruction/contrastive losses, with no connection to speech-domain SSL architectures." The latter (FreqCondNorm, SHM foundation models, MAEPD) is architecturally closer to what a leak-detection project would need, since it treats vibration/acoustic sensor data as its own modality rather than forcing it through a speech spectrogram pipeline.
- None of the found papers that name SSAST, AudioMAE, BEATs, wav2vec 2.0, HuBERT, or data2vec specifically report results on industrial acoustic/vibration anomaly detection by name in the material we could retrieve; the DCASE-ASD-adjacent papers reference "self-supervised pretrained audio models" more generically. This should be read as moderately weak evidence that these exact flagship architectures have been directly and verifiably tested on industrial sound tasks in a way we could confirm from primary sources — not as proof that no one has tried.

### Gaps
- Could not access full text of arXiv:2409.07016 or arXiv:2304.03588 (PDF fetch returned corrupted binary content both times); the specific backbone architecture(s), dataset identities, and whether evaluation was cross-dataset or within-dataset could not be confirmed.
- Could not confirm whether arXiv:2508.12230's "self-supervised pretrained models" are specifically BEATs/wav2vec2/HuBERT/WavLM (a prior fetch of the same PDF via a different pass returned those names, but that pass is suspected of being an unreliable/hallucinated summary given it contradicted the verified abstract text pulled from the arXiv abstract page — only the abstract-page text is treated as reliable here; see note in Q3).
- No confirmed evidence located either way for CLAR's application outside speech emotion recognition.
- Did not find any paper applying SSAST specifically (as opposed to AST or BEATs) to a non-speech/non-music industrial task.

## What pretrained general-purpose audio foundation models exist, and is there published evidence of them being used (frozen or fine-tuned) for non-speech/non-music classification in industrial, bioacoustic, seismic, or similar domains? Concrete examples with results.

### Takeaway
Yes — there is real, concrete, and fairly substantial published evidence for AudioSet-pretrained CNN embeddings (PANNs, VGGish, YAMNet) being used as frozen or fine-tuned feature extractors for bearing/gear fault diagnosis and for weld-defect detection, and for PANNs embeddings being used in DCASE anomalous-sound-detection pipelines. Evidence for AST, BEATs, and CLAP specifically in industrial contexts is much thinner in what we could retrieve; CLAP's zero-shot audio-language framework is well evidenced for general/bioacoustic-adjacent classification but we found no concrete industrial-sound result. AST appears used broadly for sound/event classification but we found no example specifically naming seismic or bearing-fault classification.

### Cited Findings
- PANNs (trained on AudioSet) Wavegram-Logmel-CNN embeddings (2048-dim per 90 frames for 10s clips) are used in DCASE anomalous-sound-detection pipelines: embeddings are extracted with the PANNs MobileNetV1 pretrained model, concatenated, and anomaly scores computed via cosine distance to the nearest neighbor in embedding space; PANNs is explicitly listed as an allowed external pretrained resource in DCASE challenge rules — [DCASE2022 technical report, Kodua](https://dcase.community/documents/challenge2022/technical_reports/DCASE2022_Kodua_75_t2.pdf).
- "Intelligent Fault Diagnosis of Industrial Bearings Using Transfer Learning and CNNs Pre-Trained for Audio Classification" (Sensors 2023, 23(1), 211) directly repurposes CNNs pretrained for audio classification (consistent with the VGGish/YAMNet family, both AudioSet-pretrained) for bearing fault diagnosis, framing it as transfer learning from general audio classification knowledge to vibration/bearing-sound spectrograms — [DOI:10.3390/s23010211](https://doi.org/10.3390/s23010211). We could not retrieve the full text (PMC and MDPI pages both blocked automated fetch — reCAPTCHA / 403), so dataset identity (CWRU vs. other rigs), whether train/test rigs differed, and quantitative accuracy numbers are unconfirmed from primary text; treat as a named, real, concrete example whose specifics are a gap.
- "Deep Transfer Learning for Machine Diagnosis: From Sound and Music Recognition to Bearing Fault Detection" (Applied Sciences 2021, 11(24), 11663) is another concrete, named example of transferring audio/music-recognition-pretrained CNNs to bearing fault detection — [DOI:10.3390/app112411663](https://doi.org/10.3390/app112411663) (full text not retrieved in this pass; titles/framing only).
- "Gear fault detection using noise analysis and machine learning algorithm with YAMNet pretrained network" uses Google's YAMNet (trained on AudioSet/YouTube audio) as a feature extractor for gear-fault acoustic classification — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S2214785322061211) (abstract-level confirmation only; full quantitative results not retrieved).
- "Comparative Analysis of VGGish and YAMNet Models for Welding Defect Detection" directly compares these two AudioSet-pretrained general-purpose audio embeddings as feature extractors for an industrial acoustic defect-detection task (welding), i.e., a non-speech/non-music classification problem — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/379346567_Comparative_Analysis_of_VGGish_and_YAMNet_Models_for_Welding_Defect_Detection) (abstract-level confirmation only).
- A web search summary (not independently verified against primary text) states that a YAMNet-based framework has been applied to detect bearing faults using the CWRU dataset — flagged as a secondary-source claim, not confirmed from the paper itself.
- CLAP (Contrastive Language-Audio Pretraining) is a frozen audio+text dual-encoder achieving zero-shot classification via cosine similarity between audio and class-label text embeddings, evaluated on 26 downstream audio tasks with SOTA results on several — but all tasks found in our search were general/speech/music/environmental-sound benchmarks; no industrial, bioacoustic-specific, or seismic application of CLAP was found in this search pass — [arXiv:2206.04769](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.04769), [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2206.04769).
- "Towards Open Respiratory Acoustic Foundation Models" curates ~136K respiratory audio samples (>400 hours) and pretrains a dedicated respiratory acoustic foundation model, reporting it "demonstrate[s] generalizability to unseen datasets and new audio modalities," outperforming models pretrained on general audio (AudioSet-style) — this is a biomedical-acoustic (not industrial) analogue showing domain-specific pretraining beating general-purpose pretraining for cross-dataset generalization in a non-speech/non-music domain — [NeurIPS 2024 D&B paper](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/2f803abdcad9de35b45d5a656dade45c-Paper-Datasets_and_Benchmarks_Track.pdf), [arXiv:2406.16148](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.16148).
- "DeepForestSound" is a multi-species automatic detector for passive acoustic monitoring (bioacoustics, African tropical forest) — confirms at minimum that bioacoustic classification is an active non-speech/non-music application area, though we did not confirm from the abstract whether it uses a named pretrained foundation model (e.g., AST/BEATs) as backbone or trains from scratch — [arXiv:2604.08087](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.08087) (title/topic only, not fetched for method detail).
- A civil-infrastructure SHM transfer-learning paper extracts x-vector and MFCC features using models pretrained on speaker-recognition data (e.g., VoxCeleb) and fine-tunes on bridge vibration records to classify structural damage states, explicitly leveraging "shared statistical structure between audio and structural vibrations" — this is a genuine cross-modality transfer (speech-domain pretraining to structural vibration) example, though the result characterization ("strong generalization across operational conditions") comes from a search-engine summary, not primary text we fetched directly — flagged with lower confidence.

### Inferences
- The strongest, most concrete, most-corroborated evidence base is specifically **AudioSet-pretrained CNN embeddings (VGGish, YAMNet, PANNs)** used for bearing/gear/weld acoustic fault classification. This is a good structural analogue to AcousticLeakNet's two-sensor acoustic setup: industrial machine acoustic classification using general-purpose audio embeddings as either frozen features or fine-tuning starting points. However, in every instance found, these appear to be demonstrations of transfer from general audio to one specific industrial rig/dataset — not demonstrations of transfer across independent industrial rigs or datasets (see next section).
- Transformer-based foundation audio models (AST, BEATs, CLAP) are comparatively under-evidenced for industrial/vibration use in what we could retrieve, despite being extensively benchmarked on general AudioSet/speech tasks. This may reflect either genuine absence of such evaluations in the literature, or simply gaps in this search pass — treat as a soft signal, not a confirmed absence.

### Gaps
- Could not retrieve full text (quantitative results, dataset/rig identity) for the two YAMNet/VGGish bearing-fault papers (Sensors 23(1):211 and Applied Sciences 11(24):11663) due to access blocks (403/CAPTCHA). This is a significant gap since these are the single most relevant concrete examples found.
- No confirmed example of AST, BEATs, or Whisper-encoder-as-feature-extractor applied to any industrial/vibration/seismic classification task was found in this pass.
- No confirmed example of CLAP used for industrial sound, bioacoustic, or seismic classification specifically (as opposed to its general AudioSet-style benchmark evaluation) was found.

## Is there evidence that self-supervised pretraining on one acoustic/vibration domain improves cross-dataset transfer to a genuinely different domain/rig, versus just improving within-domain data efficiency?

### Takeaway
Evidence of genuine cross-rig/cross-dataset transfer benefit is thin, and most of what superficially looks like "cross-domain evaluation" in this literature (particularly DCASE ASD "domain shift" tasks) is actually **within-machine-type domain shift** (same machine, same rig, different operating condition/section) rather than transfer across independently recorded testbeds — which is the distinction that matters for this project. The one piece of evidence we found that is explicitly described as zero-shot cross-dataset transfer (FreqCondNorm, 82.1% zero-shot accuracy transferring CWRU-pretrained-style representations to the separate MFPT bearing dataset) is encouraging but is a single paper, on tabular/time-series vibration signals (not raw audio), and was only confirmed via its abstract, not full methodology/validation review.

### Cited Findings
- FreqCondNorm reports pretraining a Transformer (masked autoencoding + contrastive learning, balanced domain sampling) jointly across five public predictive-maintenance datasets (CWRU, MFPT, UOC18, PRONOSTIA, CMAPSS) spanning sampling frequencies from 1 Hz to ~100 kHz, and reports "82.1% zero-shot accuracy on MFPT" after this multi-dataset pretraining, explicitly framed by the paper as "demonstrating strong transfer across sampling frequencies" — this is the clearest evidence found of a pretraining scheme helping genuine cross-dataset (not just cross-section-of-same-dataset) transfer for a vibration/fault-diagnosis task — [arXiv:2609.20535](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20535). Caveat: this is abstract-level confirmation only; we did not verify the paper's experimental controls (e.g., whether 82.1% is meaningfully above a from-scratch or non-pretrained baseline on MFPT, which the abstract does not state).
- The DCASE "Unsupervised Anomalous Sound Detection for Machine Condition Monitoring under Domain Shifted Conditions" challenge (DCASE 2021 task) is specifically about domain-shift robustness, but its "domain shift" refers to operating-condition changes (e.g., different speed/load/machine ID) within the same machine type and recording setup, not a fundamentally different recording rig or independent dataset — [DCASE 2021 challenge results](https://dcase.community/challenge2021/task-unsupervised-detection-of-anomalous-sounds-results). This is an important terminology trap: "domain shift" in the DCASE ASD literature is not the same claim as "cross-dataset/cross-rig transfer."
- "A Novel Cross-Sensor Transfer Diagnosis Method with Local Attention Mechanism" (applied to a reciprocating pump) frames its contribution specifically as cross-sensor transfer — i.e., using information/fault knowledge learned at one sensor location transferred to diagnosis using a different sensor's data on (it appears) the same physical pump rig, which is a narrower and more controlled claim than cross-rig/cross-dataset generalization — [PMC10490796](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10490796/) (title/framing only, not fetched for full method/result detail).
- The civil-infrastructure SHM damage-detection transfer-learning paper (arXiv:2102.03858) evaluates combinations of in-domain and cross-domain transfer learning (cross-domain there meaning ImageNet-pretrained representations transferred to a different, structural-inspection visual-image domain) across six public visual-inspection datasets, and reports the combination of cross-domain + in-domain transfer "persistently shows superior performance especially with tiny datasets" — this is a useful analogue for small-data regimes but note this paper is about **images of structural damage**, not acoustic/vibration signals, so it bears on the general principle (pretraining-plus-fine-tuning helps most when target data is scarce) rather than being a direct acoustic/vibration result — [arXiv:2102.03858](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2102.03858).
- The respiratory acoustic foundation model paper explicitly claims generalizability "to unseen datasets," a direct test of cross-dataset transfer in a non-speech/non-music acoustic domain (respiratory sounds), and claims it outperforms models pretrained on general-purpose audio (i.e., domain-specific self-supervised pretraining beat generic AudioSet-style pretraining for this cross-dataset test) — [arXiv:2406.16148](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.16148). This is one of the stronger pieces of evidence located that self-supervised pretraining genuinely helps cross-dataset transfer in an acoustic (if not industrial) domain — but it is a biomedical acoustic domain, and we did not verify the magnitude of the "unseen dataset" improvement from primary numeric results.
- Multiple general web-search summaries (treated as lower-confidence, non-primary-source claims) stated that cross-dataset evaluation in audio-based detection systems "reveals severe degradation when models are evaluated on datasets different from their training data, highlighting fundamental challenges in developing generalizable audio-based detection systems" — this is consistent with, and corroborates, this project's own prior concern that within-domain gains do not reliably transfer; however this specific claim could not be traced to one identifiable primary paper in this pass, so it is reported as a pattern noted across secondary sources rather than a single citable finding.
- Regarding arXiv:2508.12230 (self-supervised audio models for ASD): the verified abstract (fetched directly from the arXiv abstract page) states evaluation was performed "on all benchmark datasets, including the DCASE 2020–2024 five ASD challenges" — again, DCASE ASD benchmark-to-benchmark comparison, which involves different machine types across years but all drawn from the same broader DCASE ASD benchmark family/recording conventions, not independently sourced industrial rigs in the way "Mendeley testbed vs. our own synthetic/EPANET generator" would be for this project. We flag that an earlier, different fetch attempt on the same PDF returned a summary explicitly claiming the paper "provides evidence distinguishing between within-domain robustness and authentic cross-dataset generalization" and demonstrates transfer to "previously unseen industrial equipment scenarios" — but this claim is NOT corroborated by the verified abstract text, which makes no such explicit claim and does not name specific backbone models. Per our source-evaluation standard, we treat the second, more specific-sounding summary as unreliable (likely over-interpretation or confabulation by the summarizing pass) and rely only on the verified abstract wording above.

### Inferences
- Across this entire search, we found exactly one piece of evidence (FreqCondNorm) that looks like a genuine, named, quantified instance of pretraining improving transfer to an independently-sourced dataset in a vibration/fault-diagnosis setting, and even that is a single abstract-level claim we could not fully audit. Everything else we found either (a) is within-domain (same rig, same machine type, different section/operating point, as in DCASE ASD "domain shift"), (b) is a general claim without quantitative cross-dataset numbers, or (c) is from an adjacent but not identical domain (bioacoustic/respiratory, SHM images).
- This pattern is consistent with — and does not contradict — the project's working concern: there is not a strong, well-replicated literature showing that self-supervised/foundation-model pretraining reliably transfers across genuinely independent acoustic/vibration recording rigs for anomaly/fault-style binary classification. The clearest documented gains in this literature are either within-domain (same dataset family, data-efficiency/robustness to known operating-condition shift) or are claims made at the abstract level without the kind of rigorous, bootstrapped, recording-level cross-rig validation this project already holds itself to.
- Given the project's own setup (synthetic EPANET+acoustic-synthesizer training data, evaluated on an independently recorded Mendeley testbed), the literature's own experience suggests caution: even in domains with more publications and larger foundation models (DCASE ASD, bearing fault diagnosis), robust demonstrated cross-rig transfer is rare and under-quantified. Where it is claimed, it is in either (i) narrower cross-sensor-same-rig settings, or (ii) single new papers (FreqCondNorm, respiratory foundation model) whose transfer claims we could not fully audit from abstracts alone.

### Gaps
- Could not verify FreqCondNorm's 82.1% zero-shot MFPT result against a credible non-pretrained baseline number, nor confirm from primary text whether "zero-shot" means literally no fine-tuning on MFPT labels at all, or some lighter adaptation.
- Could not find any paper explicitly pretraining on synthetic/simulated acoustic or vibration data and testing transfer to real recorded data (the exact structure of this project's own train/test split) — this is a direct and significant gap; we found no synthetic-to-real transfer literature specifically for self-supervised acoustic/vibration pretraining in industrial/leak-detection-adjacent settings.
- Could not independently confirm or refute the secondary-source claim of "severe degradation" under cross-dataset evaluation of audio-based detection systems back to one specific primary paper.
- Given two failed PDF fetches (2409.07016, 2304.03588) and one paper we could not access past a CAPTCHA/403 wall (Sensors 23(1):211), there is real residual uncertainty about whether stronger cross-domain transfer evidence exists in the industrial-ASD/bearing-fault literature that this pass simply could not retrieve.

## What are realistic compute/data requirements to pretrain or fine-tune such models, given small real datasets (tens–low hundreds of recordings per source) plus an effectively unlimited synthetic generator?

### Takeaway
We found comparatively little direct, quantified information on GPU-hours/data-volume requirements specific to pretraining or fine-tuning these architectures for industrial acoustic/vibration tasks; what we did find supports a general, lower-confidence directional picture: (1) parameter-efficient fine-tuning (LoRA, adapters) of an already-pretrained general-audio model is explicitly used in this literature specifically because full fine-tuning on small industrial datasets overfits, and (2) foundation-model approaches to industrial/vibration tasks tend to pretrain jointly across multiple existing public datasets (not from a single lab's small data) to get enough volume — which maps reasonably well to this project's synthetic-data-generator advantage, but we have no citable numeric compute-hour figures.

### Cited Findings
- The ASD paper (arXiv:2508.12230) explicitly motivates its use of LoRA ("Fully-Connected Low-Rank Adaptation") as "an alternative to full fine-tuning" specifically "to mitigate overfitting and retain learned knowledge when fine-tuning with limited data" — directly confirming that full fine-tuning of large pretrained audio models on small industrial ASD datasets is considered risky/overfitting-prone in this literature, and that parameter-efficient fine-tuning is the field's preferred mitigation — [arXiv:2508.12230 abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.12230).
- FreqCondNorm's pretraining strategy is to combine five separate public predictive-maintenance datasets (CWRU, MFPT, UOC18, PRONOSTIA, CMAPSS) with "balanced domain sampling" specifically to get enough pretraining data/diversity across sampling frequencies spanning 1 Hz–100 kHz — i.e., the realistic path taken in this adjacent literature is pooling multiple existing small public datasets rather than needing one enormous dataset, which is a soft analogue for pooling this project's own synthetic data with any available public datasets (Hong Kong/Dongguan/Sheffield per this project's own E9/E10 experiments) — [arXiv:2609.20535](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20535).
- COLA pretrains on the full AudioSet (~1.8M 10-second clips, ~5,000 hours), which is the scale associated with general-purpose contrastive audio representation learning success in its original domain — this is an order-of-magnitude reference point (millions of clips) for what "large-scale" SSL audio pretraining typically means, useful as context for how far this project's synthetic generator (or its public-dataset additions) would be from "foundation model scale" — [arXiv:2010.10915](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2010.10915).
- No GPU-hour, wall-clock, or hardware-cost figures for pretraining or fine-tuning any of SSAST/AudioMAE/BEATs/wav2vec2/HuBERT/data2vec/COLA were found in any source retrieved in this pass.

### Inferences
- Given that this project already has an "effectively unlimited" synthetic generator (EPANET hydraulics + physics-based acoustic synthesizer) but only small real-world datasets, the literature's own revealed preference — parameter-efficient fine-tuning (LoRA/adapters) on top of pretraining, rather than full from-scratch pretraining on the small real set — suggests the more literature-consistent path (if self-supervised pretraining is pursued at all) would be: self-supervised/contrastive pretraining on the large synthetic corpus (where "large-scale" is achievable), then light fine-tuning (ideally parameter-efficient) on the small real Mendeley recordings, rather than attempting to pretrain an SSL model directly on the tens-to-hundreds of real recordings. This directly mirrors FreqCondNorm's multi-dataset-pooling strategy and the ASD-LoRA papers' small-data-overfitting rationale.
- However, this inference should be weighed against the Q3 finding: the one concrete piece of evidence for pretraining improving cross-dataset transfer (FreqCondNorm) involved pretraining across *multiple real datasets*, not synthetic-to-real transfer. We found zero direct evidence either confirming or ruling out that synthetic-data self-supervised pretraining followed by fine-tuning on small real recordings actually improves cross-rig generalization versus simply training a supervised model directly on the synthetic data (this project's current approach) — this is the single most important open question for this project's specific use case, and the literature search did not resolve it.

### Gaps
- No quantified GPU-hour, dataset-size-vs-performance curve, or compute-budget guidance was found for any of the specifically named SSL architectures (SSAST, AudioMAE, BEATs, wav2vec 2.0, HuBERT, data2vec) in an industrial/vibration context.
- No paper was found that specifically addresses the question "how much synthetic self-supervised pretraining data is needed before fine-tuning transfers well to a small real dataset" for acoustic/vibration anomaly detection — this is a genuine, unresolved gap directly relevant to this project's design choice.
- We did not find cost/compute guidance distinguishing masked-spectrogram-modeling pretraining compute cost vs. contrastive-pretraining compute cost specifically for vibration/industrial time series (as opposed to the well-documented costs for the original speech/AudioSet versions of these methods, which we did not independently pull given they are off-topic for the industrial-transfer question).
