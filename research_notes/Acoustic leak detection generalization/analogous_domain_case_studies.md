# Analogous-Domain Case Studies: Cross-Dataset / Cross-Rig Generalization in Acoustic & Vibration Anomaly Detection

## Key Question 1: In bearing/gearbox fault diagnosis, which papers test cross-machine or cross-dataset transfer (e.g. CWRU → Paderborn/MFPT/IMS/JNU), what methods were used, and what is the transfer performance vs in-domain?

### Takeaway
Genuine cross-dataset/cross-rig transfer in bearing fault diagnosis is well studied, but essentially no paper achieves high accuracy with *zero* target-domain labels on a truly different rig — every successful case uses at least a handful of labeled target samples (few-shot) or large amounts of unlabeled target data (adversarial/semi-supervised domain adaptation). Pure source-only transfer (no adaptation, no target labels) typically lands around 54–80% accuracy on a 2–4 class problem, versus 92–99.95% after adaptation with a few labeled target examples — i.e., the raw cross-rig domain gap is large and is closed only with some target supervision, not none.

### Cited Findings
- A Siamese-network few-shot method trained source-only (CWRU, artificially seeded inner/outer-race/normal faults, 12 kHz) and evaluated with no adaptation on Paderborn University (naturally damaged bearings from accelerated lifetime testing, 64 kHz) achieved only ~54% accuracy ("S(s)", direct transfer baseline) — [A Novel Bearing Fault Diagnosis Method Based on Few-Shot Transfer Learning across Different Datasets](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9497688/)
- The same paper's best variant, which replaces the support set with just 5 labeled target-domain (Paderborn) samples per class ("S(t)", 5-shot), reached 96.53% average accuracy across six transfer tasks (A→D, B→D, C→D, A→E, B→E, C→E), with std. dev. 2.66% — [same source](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9497688/)
- This CWRU→Paderborn setup is explicitly cross-dataset and cross-machine: different institutions, different sampling rates (12 kHz vs 64 kHz), and different fault-generation methods (artificially seeded vs naturally grown faults) — [same source](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9497688/)
- A semi-supervised discriminative adversarial domain adaptation (adversarial domain-adversarial training + batch nuclear-norm maximization + virtual adversarial training) study used three genuinely different rigs — CWRU (12 kHz, deep-groove ball bearings, 1797 rpm), IMS/Univ. of Cincinnati (20 kHz, double-row spherical roller bearings, 2000 rpm), and JNU/Jiangnan University (50 kHz, single-row spherical roller bearings, 1000 rpm) — noting the bearings "show significant difference between each other" — [Cross-Machine Fault Diagnosis with Semi-Supervised Discriminative Adversarial Domain Adaptation](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7374333/)
- With only 1 labeled target sample per class, plain DANN (adversarial domain adaptation alone) scored 80.47% (CWRU→IMS), 63.82% (CWRU→JNU), 77.85% (IMS→CWRU), and 67.48% (IMS→JNU); the full proposed method raised these to 99.95%, 92.07%, 85.85%, and 81.41% respectively, averaging 97.86% across all tested transfer pairs — [same source](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7374333/)
- A broader literature base exists on unsupervised deep transfer learning for cross-domain fault diagnosis (surveyed across CWRU/other rig pairs), reflecting that this is an active, well-funded research niche precisely because naive cross-rig transfer fails — [Applications of Unsupervised Deep Transfer Learning to Intelligent Fault Diagnosis: A Survey and Comparative Study](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1912.12528)

### Inferences
- The consistent pattern (large accuracy collapse with source-only transfer; strong recovery only once model gets a few labeled or a lot of unlabeled target-domain data) suggests that bearing acoustics/vibration signatures are not naturally rig-invariant, and success stories in this literature are success stories for *domain adaptation algorithms with target-domain access*, not for pure generalization. For a project like ours where only synthetic-source and un-labeled/no-access real target data exist, the more apt comparison is the unadapted baseline numbers (54-80%), not the adapted ones (85-99%).
- Few-shot/semi-supervised methods in this field assume the target rig's data is available for adaptation (even if unlabeled or weakly labeled); a fully blind train-on-source/deploy-on-target setup (our setup, if no Mendeley labels are ever used) is the harder, less-published case.

### Gaps
- No paper found reporting literal zero-shot (no target data at all, not even unlabeled) cross-rig bearing fault classification performance as a clean baseline across many rig pairs — most papers report either "source-only" baselines folded into their adaptation comparison tables (as above) rather than as a standalone generalization study.
- MFPT-specific cross-dataset numbers were not independently retrieved in this pass (MFPT appeared in search snippets as part of multi-dataset training pools but no specific train-A/test-B number was extracted with a citation).

## Key Question 2: In structural health monitoring, is there published work on cross-structure or cross-sensor-placement generalization of acoustic/vibration damage detection?

### Takeaway
Yes — cross-structure SHM transfer is an active sub-field (guided-wave and vibration-based damage detection/quantification across different structures), but this pass could not retrieve full quantitative results for most candidate papers due to paywalls; the existence and framing of these papers confirms that cross-structure generalization is treated as a hard, unsolved problem requiring explicit transfer-learning machinery, not something that "just works."

### Cited Findings
- A method titled "Distribution adaptation deep transfer learning for cross-structure health monitoring using guided waves" explicitly frames the problem as follows: monitoring expertise learned on structure A is difficult to apply to structure B "due to differences in the dispersion and receiving modes of different waveguides," and proposes joint distribution adaptation (adapting both marginal and conditional distributions of guided-wave signals) to bridge genuinely different structures — [Distribution adaptation deep transfer learning method for cross-structure health monitoring using guided waves](https://doi.org/10.1177/14759217211010709) (full text paywalled; quantitative results not independently verified in this pass)
- A "Damage Quantification Monitoring of CFRP Structures Cross-Area Based on Transfer Learning" paper proposes a multi-scale adaptive cross-area damage-quantification method, explicitly minimizing source/target domain differences and using a Bidirectional GRU for damage quantification across structurally distinct zones — [PMC13517379](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13517379/) / [Sensors 26,16,5068](https://doi.org/10.3390/s26165068)
- "Damage detection using in-domain and cross-domain transfer learning" explores transferring a model trained on one structure to another without damage-sensitive hand-engineered features, using full-spectrum FFT amplitudes "to help generalizability" — framed as a genuine cross-structure transfer problem — [arXiv:2102.03858](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2102.03858)
- "Zero-Shot Transfer Learning for Structural Health Monitoring using Generative Adversarial Networks and Spectral Mapping" targets the harder zero-target-data case (by name), implying that non-zero-shot cross-structure transfer is the norm and zero-shot is explicitly flagged as a distinct, harder contribution — [arXiv:2212.04002](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.04002)
- "Cross-domain structural damage identification using transfer learning strategy" is a further paper in this space (abstract-only visibility in this pass) — [ScienceDirect S0141029624007338](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0141029624007338)

### Inferences
- The framing language across all five papers ("difficult to apply," "major challenge," naming zero-shot as a special/harder case) indicates the SHM field treats cross-structure generalization the same way the bearing-fault field does: as something that fails without explicit adaptation machinery, and that even adaptation methods are evaluated structure-pair by structure-pair rather than shown to generalize broadly.

### Gaps
- Quantitative in-domain vs cross-structure accuracy numbers could not be retrieved (SagePub and ScienceDirect blocked full-text fetch with 403 errors in this pass); a report-writer wanting exact percentages should access these papers directly via institutional access.
- No paper was found addressing cross-sensor-placement generalization specifically (same structure, different sensor location) as distinct from cross-structure generalization — this narrower sub-question remains unanswered.

## Key Question 3: In pipeline leak detection (water or gas) specifically, has any paper tested a model across two independently collected real datasets/rigs rather than a single testbed's train/test split?

### Takeaway
This is the most directly relevant finding: yes, at least two 2025/2026 Water Research papers explicitly test acoustic (or acoustic-adjacent) leak-detection models across genuinely different, independently collected real water-distribution networks — and both report a real, non-trivial transfer gap that is only partly closed with fine-tuning on target data, strongly reinforcing that cross-rig generalization is an open, actively-being-measured problem in exactly this domain, not a solved one. No comparable cross-rig acoustic emission study was found for gas pipelines — that side of the literature remains almost entirely single-testbed, within-dataset-split work.

### Cited Findings
- "Evaluating the generalizability and transferability of acoustic leak detection models for water distribution networks" (Water Research, vol. 286, 2025) systematically evaluates acoustic leak-detection (ALD) models across **multi-source/multi-region real water distribution networks** that differ in pipe material and diameter, explicitly stating that "cross-domain deployment of single-scenario ALD models is significantly hindered by environmental heterogeneity ... and data scarcity in practical WDNs" — [ScienceDirect S0043135425011790](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0043135425011790) (abstract/snippet-level access only; full text 403-blocked in this pass)
- In that paper, a **global model trained across multiple regions** generalizes only ~2 percentage points better than a **local model** trained on a single region/network — a small improvement, implying raw multi-source training does not by itself solve cross-site generalization — [same source, via search snippet](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0043135425011790)
- Direct cross-domain transfer (no adaptation) and feature-extraction transfer both underperform a **Fine-Tuning** strategy, which reaches 96.8% (cross-material) and 96.2% (cross-diameter) accuracy — and even with fine-tuning, target-domain labeled-data requirements could only be cut by ~50% versus a target-only local model, meaning substantial target labels are still needed, not zero — [same source, via search snippet](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0043135425011790)
- "Cross-regional leak detection in water distribution networks through domain transfer" (Water Research, Feb 2026, DOI 10.1016/j.watres.2025.125150) is a collaboration between Dalian University of Technology and Hong Kong Polytechnic University and explicitly tests a domain-adaptation framework transferring a leak-detection model between **Hong Kong and Dalian** — two geographically and administratively independent real networks — reporting **>10 percentage points higher accuracy than baseline** (i.e., than naive/no-adaptation cross-region transfer) after domain adaptation — [PubMed 41401765](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41401765/) (full text not accessible in this pass; based on search-engine-indexed abstract text, not independently verified against the primary PDF)
- Caveat on modality: search context for the Hong Kong/Dalian paper co-occurred heavily with pressure/SCADA-sensor leak-detection literature rather than acoustic-sensor literature; this pass could not confirm whether that paper uses acoustic sensing or pressure/flow (SCADA) signals — treat the ">10%" figure as a cross-site infrastructure-anomaly-detection result, with the acoustic-specific claim unverified — [PubMed 41401765](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41401765/)
- For gas pipelines, extensive search did not surface any paper testing a model trained on one acoustic-emission rig and evaluated (with or without adaptation) on an independently collected second rig/dataset. All retrieved gas-pipeline acoustic-emission papers (CWT+CNN, DenseNet, Inception-v3, CNN-LSTM, DBN-GA-LSSVM) report single-testbed accuracy (often 97–99.8%) with only narrative acknowledgment that "generalization capabilities" are limited, not a measured cross-rig number — [Acoustic Emission-Based Pipeline Leak Detection and Size Identification Using a Customized One-Dimensional DenseNet](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11858961/), [A Method for Pipeline Leak Detection Based on Acoustic Imaging and Deep Learning](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8875737/)

### Inferences
- The water-pipeline-specific evidence is the strongest direct analog in this whole research set to our project's exact problem (train on synthetic/testbed source, test on an independently collected real target): even within the same general application domain (real water distribution acoustic leak detection), cross-site transfer without target labels is explicitly reported as hindered, and the literature's own "solutions" require either fine-tuning on target labels (still only halving, not eliminating, the labeled-data need) or multi-region training that yields only a small (~2 pt) generalization bump.
- No pipeline paper of any kind (water or gas) was found demonstrating strong zero-target-label cross-rig/cross-dataset performance — the closest thing (fine-tuning results) still assumes labeled target access, which is a materially easier setting than evaluating a frozen synthetic-trained model on unseen real recordings.

### Gaps
- Full-text access to both key Water Research papers was blocked (403) in this pass; the quantitative figures above come from search-engine-rendered abstract/snippet text, not a verified read of the primary source — a follow-up with institutional/library access to ScienceDirect and the Feb 2026 Water Research issue is recommended before citing exact percentages in a manuscript.
- Could not confirm whether the Hong Kong/Dalian paper's sensing modality is acoustic (vs. pressure/SCADA); this materially affects how directly comparable it is to our acoustic leak detector.
- No gas-pipeline cross-rig acoustic emission study was found at all — this appears to be a genuine gap in the published literature, not just a search-coverage failure (multiple phrasings tried, all returning only within-testbed results).

## Key Question 4: In underwater acoustics and seismic event classification, is there evidence of models that generalize across independently collected recording campaigns/sensor networks for a similar binary/few-class event detection task?

### Takeaway
Underwater acoustics provides the clearest, most quantitatively documented *negative* result in this entire research set: a model trained on one labeled hydrophone/archive dataset and evaluated zero-shot on an independently collected dataset suffers a catastrophic accuracy collapse (96% in-domain to as low as 15% cross-domain), only partially recovered (to ~52%) with heavy domain-adaptation machinery — still far below in-domain performance. Seismic phase-picking shows a more encouraging picture, but the strongest "generalization" results come from models trained on already-huge, already-multi-network aggregated datasets (e.g., STEAD), which is a weaker claim than true independent-dataset transfer; the one genuinely independent cross-sensor-type case found (land seismometers → ocean-bottom seismometers) required explicit fine-tuning on newly auto-labeled target data and still reports only partial recall recovery at long range, with no clean in-domain OBS upper-bound comparison available.

### Cited Findings
- A Tiny-CNN trained on USS8 (1,099 manually curated 1-second clips from an open-source maritime sound archive; 8 classes including whale, ship, sonar, torpedo, etc.) achieved 96.35% accuracy / 0.965 macro-F1 **in-domain**, but when evaluated zero-shot on ShipsEar (field recordings from the port of Vigo, Spain — a genuinely independently collected dataset, collapsed to binary ship/ambient) with plain cross-entropy training, performance fell to just 15.31% overall accuracy and 5.91% ship-detection rate — an 80+ percentage point collapse — [Underwater Source Detection and Classification for Signal-based Surveillance: Audio Dataset Curation and Cross-Domain Evaluation](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.28988v2)
- Adding a margin-enhanced loss (CE-PlusPairMargin) raised ship-detection to 29.43%; adding feature-statistics alignment (matching source/target distributions at inference time) raised it further to 48.51% ship-detection / 51.86% overall accuracy on ShipsEar — still roughly 45 points below the in-domain figure despite two layers of mitigation — [same source](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.28988v2)
- A large-scale pretraining dataset, Oceanship (107,540 audio-text pairs, >230x larger than prior UATR datasets), has been proposed specifically to improve cross-domain/generalization performance for underwater acoustic target recognition, implying the field recognizes current datasets (DeepShip ~47 hrs, ShipsEar ~3h8m, Watkins marine-mammal corpus) are too small/narrow for models to generalize across campaigns on their own — [Oceanship: A Large-Scale Dataset for Underwater Audio Target Recognition](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2401.02099)
- For seismic event/phase classification, the STEAD dataset itself already aggregates ~1,050,000 three-component waveforms from 2,613 seismometers worldwide across ~450,000 events — meaning models "generalizing to unseen stations" within this ecosystem (e.g., PhaseNO) are generalizing within an already highly diverse, multi-network training pool, not from one single independently-collected dataset to a wholly separate one — [Phase Neural Operator for Multi-Station Picking of Seismic Arrivals](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1029/2023GL106434)
- The one clearly independent cross-sensor-type seismic case found, OBSTransformer, fine-tunes a land-seismic-network-trained model (EqTransformer) onto a newly compiled, genuinely distinct target domain — ~36,000 earthquake + ~25,000 noise samples from 423 ocean-bottom seismometers (OBS) across 11 temporary worldwide deployments — using automated labeling (a 5-picker consensus: EqTransformer, PhaseNet, GPD, PickNet, AIC) rather than human labels, then fine-tuning — [OBSTransformer: A Deep-Learning Seismic Phase Picker for OBS Data Using Automated Labelling and Transfer Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.04753)
- After this fine-tuning transfer, P- and S-phase recall at long range (>200 km) improved by 68% and 76% respectively over the un-adapted land-trained baseline applied directly to OBS data — but the source paper (per this pass's retrieval) does not report a clean in-domain-OBS-trained upper bound or F1/precision, so the residual gap to a "trained natively on OBS data" ceiling is unknown — [same source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.04753)

### Inferences
- The underwater-acoustics finding (96% → 15% collapse, partial recovery to ~52%) is arguably the single most important analogous data point for our project: it is a clean, well-isolated two-sensor/passive-acoustic binary-ish classification task (ship vs. ambient), trained on one independently collected corpus and tested zero-shot on another, and it shows that even with modern domain-adaptation techniques (margin loss + feature alignment), cross-dataset acoustic event classification remains far below in-domain performance. This is a strong, citable precedent for treating our unsolved cross-dataset problem as a known-hard, field-general phenomenon rather than a shortcoming specific to our pipeline.
- The seismic evidence is more mixed/optimistic-looking on the surface, but the strongest "generalizes to new stations" claims rely on training on an already-massive, pre-aggregated multi-network dataset (STEAD) — which is a fundamentally different (and much easier) setting than our single-synthetic-source-to-single-real-target problem. The genuinely analogous case (OBSTransformer, land→OBS) still required target-domain data collection, automated labeling, and fine-tuning — it was not zero-shot — and even then only partial recall recovery is reported.

### Gaps
- No in-domain OBS-trained upper-bound number was found for OBSTransformer, so the true residual domain gap after fine-tuning (vs. what an OBS-native model would achieve) could not be quantified.
- The underwater "Cross-Domain Knowledge Transfer for Underwater Acoustic Classification Using Pre-trained Models" paper (arXiv:2409.13878, comparing PANNs vs. ImageNet-pretrained backbones) could not be read in enough detail in this pass (PDF/abstract fetch returned only a partial summary) to extract its specific datasets and numbers — flagged as a probable additional relevant source for a follow-up pass.
- No seismic paper was found that cleanly isolates "trained only on network/campaign A, zero-shot tested on fully independent campaign B" with both baselines and an in-domain ceiling reported side by side — the literature's framing of "generalization" in this field is almost always within an already-pooled multi-source training set, which this pass flags explicitly as a weaker claim than the report's filter calls for.
