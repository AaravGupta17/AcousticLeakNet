# Real leak data stays scarce; transfer stays unsolved

The research turned up no new, independently downloadable, multi-class real-world water-pipe acoustic leak dataset beyond the four AcousticLeakNet already uses — the most promising new leads are either confidential (a large Sydney utility deployment), format-degraded (a South Korean corpus released only as FFT spectra, not raw audio), or likely duplicates of data the project already has (a 2026 Dongguan Zenodo release sited in the same city as the project's existing Dongguan source). On the methods side, the literature across five independent analogous domains — bearing/gearbox fault diagnosis, structural health monitoring, pipeline leak detection itself, underwater acoustics, and seismology — converges on the same hard conclusion: no study found achieves strong performance transferring a model trained on one acoustic/vibration rig to a genuinely independent rig with **zero labeled target data**. Every successful cross-dataset result in this review required at least a handful of labeled target examples, substantial unlabeled target data for adaptation, or both; raw "train on A, deploy blind on B" accuracy routinely collapses by 20–80 percentage points. This means AcousticLeakNet's core challenge — synthetic/confounded-source to genuinely unseen real target, with little or no target labels — is not a gap in its own engineering but the unsolved frontier of the entire field. The most defensible next steps are narrower than "find more data" or "add a foundation model": they are to (1) treat the single-class-domain confound in the project's own real sources as a first-order methodological problem that published domain-adaptation methods do not address out of the box, and (2) budget for at least some genuinely labeled or auto-labeled target-domain data, because nothing in the literature suggests a path to strong zero-label transfer without it.

## No independent real water-leak dataset surfaced beyond the existing four

Cross-checking primary sources confirmed that three of the project's four existing real datasets have traceable origin papers: the Aghashahi/Sela/Banks PVC testbed (*Data in Brief* 48, 109148, 2023; DOI 10.17632/tbrnp6vrnj.1 via Mendeley, CC BY, with at least two earlier companion Mendeley releases from the same 47 m/152.4 mm testbed under different DOIs — [PMC10147960](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10147960/), [Mendeley Data](https://data.mendeley.com/datasets/tbrnp6vrnj/1)), the Sheffield MDPE calibration rig (Shekofteh et al., *Sensors* 2026, 26, 4325; DOI 10.3390/s26144325, deposited at Sheffield's ORDA/Figshare repository — [Sensors paper](https://www.mdpi.com/1424-8220/26/14/4325)), and almost certainly the Hong Kong dataset (Tijani et al., "Acoustic Based Data Acquisition for Leak Detection of Water Distribution Networks," Mendeley DOI via hkn8mxcjyz, ~90 real leak sites over 12 months, noise loggers/hydrophones/MEMS accelerometers on metallic and non-metallic pipe — [Mendeley Data](https://data.mendeley.com/datasets/hkn8mxcjyz/1)). A 2026 Zenodo release from Guangdong University of Technology's Dongguan "outdoor leakage detection training base" (DOI 10.5281/zenodo.18631450, CC BY 4.0, 500 leak/386 no-leak/114 noise 1-second clips) is the strongest *candidate* for being new, but shares the same city and training-base framing as the project's existing Dongguan source and could not be confirmed as independent — this needs a direct check against the project's current citation before being counted as a fifth data point ([Zenodo record](https://zenodo.org/records/18631450)).

Beyond these, the most ecologically valid real-world evidence found — a multi-year, six-area commercial "Lift and Shift" vibro-acoustic monitoring deployment across metropolitan Sydney, with ~70% of detected leaks being pre-existing/hidden real field leaks rather than induced ones, and CNN+STFT classification reported above 94% accuracy — is explicitly **not publicly available**; the papers state the raw dataset "cannot be made publicly available due to confidentiality," with access only via contacting the corresponding author ([Sensors 2022, PMC9504692](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9504692/), [Vibration 2022](https://doi.org/10.3390/vibration5020021)). A large, real, in-situ vibration corpus from South Korea's AI Hub portal (~30,000 labeled cases across 11,000+ sensor locations in Gwangju and Goheung, two sensor types, outdoor/indoor leak plus noise categories) is the single most promising lead for new volume, but the published data format is FFT magnitude spectra rather than raw time-domain waveform, and the portal itself returned a 403 on direct fetch, leaving license terms and international-account access unverified ([MDPI Sensors 23(21):8935](https://www.mdpi.com/1424-8220/23/21/8935)). GPLA-12, a real (not simulated) acoustic-emission dataset with public GitHub releases, is the field's rare case of a genuinely open, versioned leak-acoustic dataset — but it is **gas pipeline**, not water, so its physics (compressible-flow orifice acoustics, no hydrophone/liquid-coupling behavior) differ enough that it is useful only for architecture/methodology transfer, not as real-world water validation data ([arXiv:2106.10277](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.10277), [GitHub](https://github.com/Deep-AI-Application-DAIP/acoustic-leakage-dataset-GPLA-12)). Several other Chinese-city datasets (an "HZ city" 12,316-sample set, a 1,004-sample WDN set, a cast-iron/steel/concrete Jiangsu-Zhejiang-Shanghai acoustic-emission set) surfaced only as uncorroborated search-snippet fragments inside a 2026 IEEE survey and one *Water* 2022 paper whose full text could not be fetched; these remain unconfirmed and should not be treated as accessible datasets until someone reads the primary PDFs directly ([survey](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/abstract/document/11408040), [Water 2022](https://doi.org/10.3390/w14243992)). The structural pattern across this whole search — one open gas-pipeline dataset, one open small Chinese water-leak release, and otherwise either confidential utility data or second-hand unverifiable claims — indicates that real-world acoustic leak data is **systematically withheld or under-released** across the field, not merely hard to find by search; this is consistent with, not incidental to, the difficulty this project has already had sourcing field data. No multi-material (PVC + ductile iron + steel + PE + asbestos cement + concrete), both-class, publicly downloadable water dataset was found at all — every verified open real dataset is single-material (PVC for Aghashahi, MDPE for Sheffield).

## Domain generalization methods assume a structure AcousticLeakNet's real sources don't have

The standard toolkit — DANN's adversarial feature alignment ([JMLR 2016](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/volume17/15-239/15-239.pdf)), Deep CORAL's covariance matching ([ECCV 2016](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-319-49409-8_35)), MMD/DAN-style statistical alignment, IRM, and meta-learning DG variants (MLDG, Meta-GENE, AdaMETA) — has been extensively re-implemented and benchmarked specifically on vibration/bearing time-series data via the public `TL-Fault-Diagnosis-Library`, which covers CWRU, MFPT, PU, XJTU, IMS, and JNU bearing datasets with cross-dataset (not just cross-condition) evaluation protocols ([GitHub](https://github.com/Feaxure-fresh/TL-Fault-Diagnosis-Library)). This is the closest off-the-shelf, pre-built, same-modality (1D vibration/time-series) codebase found for AcousticLeakNet to adapt, and is a more defensible starting point than porting vision-benchmark DG code. IRM specifically carries a well-documented theoretical warning: Rosenfeld & Ravikumar prove it recovers the true invariant predictor only when the number of observed environments exceeds the dimensionality of environment-varying features, and a feasible non-invariant solution can achieve *lower* empirical risk than the true invariant one and get selected instead ([arXiv:2010.05761](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2010.05761)); a companion paper shows IRM's practical linear form fails to capture invariance even on toy problems built from its own motivating examples ([arXiv:2101.01134](https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.01134)). With at most a handful of true "environments" (synthetic generator plus two or three real recording sites), AcousticLeakNet sits squarely in the regime where IRM is documented to fail, and DANN/CORAL/MMD — which at least have direct empirical validation on vibration cross-dataset transfer — are the safer bet.

The single most important critical finding from this pass, however, is structural rather than algorithmic: **every DG/DA method surveyed assumes each source/target domain contains a mix of both classes**, so its alignment loss can be computed class-conditionally or at minimum evaluated per-domain. AcousticLeakNet's real sources violate this outright — every real site is single-class by construction (e.g., Mendeley Branched is all contaminated no-leak background, per the project's own documented confound in `docs/CLAIMS.md`/`docs/INTEGRITY_LOG.md`). No source found in this search explicitly studies or benchmarks DG/DA under this exact confound; the closest related work (within-domain class imbalance, one-class-vs-open-set anomaly detection, semi-supervised DG with unlabeled-but-not-single-class domains) each address a different problem. The practical implication, inferred but not directly sourced, is that class-conditional alignment can only be computed using the synthetic domain (which is balanced and multi-class), while the single-class real domains can only serve as unlabeled targets for *marginal* statistics matching — CORAL/MMD on global feature statistics, or AdaBN-style batch-norm statistics adaptation as used in acoustic-scene-classification UDA ([arXiv:2005.00145](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2005.00145)). Two popular test-time adaptation methods are actively dangerous under this structure: Tent's entropy minimization and SHOT's pseudo-labeling both reinforce a model's own confident predictions within a batch ([Tent, arXiv:2006.10726](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.10726); [SHOT, arXiv:2002.08546](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2002.08546)) — in an all-no-leak target domain, a confidently wrong "leak" prediction has no counter-signal to correct it, since there are no true leak examples present to push back. This is a direct mechanical consequence of how both methods are defined, not a hypothetical, and should gate any decision to apply either method naively to the project's class-imbalanced, single-class real sources.

## Foundation-model pretraining offers architecture, not proof of transfer

Self-supervised audio architectures developed for speech/music (SSAST, AudioMAE, BEATs, wav2vec 2.0, HuBERT, data2vec, COLA, CLAP) have thin, mostly indirect evidence of application to industrial acoustic/vibration tasks; AudioSet-pretrained CNN embeddings (VGGish, YAMNet, PANNs) have much more concrete, if narrower, precedent — used as frozen or fine-tuned features for bearing-fault diagnosis ([Sensors 23(1):211](https://doi.org/10.3390/s23010211); [Applied Sciences 11(24):11663](https://doi.org/10.3390/app112411663)), gear-fault detection with YAMNet ([ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S2214785322061211)), welding-defect detection comparing VGGish vs. YAMNet ([ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/379346567_Comparative_Analysis_of_VGGish_and_YAMNet_Models_for_Welding_Defect_Detection)), and PANNs embeddings inside DCASE anomalous-sound-detection pipelines ([DCASE2022 technical report](https://dcase.community/documents/challenge2022/technical_reports/DCASE2022_Kodua_75_t2.pdf)). A separate, architecturally more relevant thread pretrains masked-autoencoder/contrastive transformers directly on numeric vibration time series rather than repurposing speech spectrograms: FreqCondNorm pretrains jointly across five public predictive-maintenance datasets (CWRU, MFPT, UOC18, PRONOSTIA, CMAPSS) spanning 1 Hz–100 kHz sampling and reports **82.1% zero-shot accuracy on MFPT** after this multi-dataset pretraining, the clearest single piece of evidence in this entire review of pretraining aiding genuine cross-dataset transfer in a vibration/fault-diagnosis task — though this is an abstract-level claim not yet audited against a from-scratch baseline ([arXiv:2609.20535](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20535)). "Foundation Models for Structural Health Monitoring" applies the same MAE-pretraining principle to bridge/viaduct vibration data ([arXiv:2404.02944](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.02944)), and a respiratory-acoustic foundation model trained on ~136,000 samples explicitly claims domain-specific pretraining beats generic AudioSet-style pretraining for cross-dataset generalization in a non-speech acoustic domain ([NeurIPS 2024 D&B](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.16148)) — the strongest acoustic (if non-industrial) analogue for "pretrain on your own modality, not borrowed speech features."

Critically, a crucial distinction recurred throughout this literature that should temper optimism: much of what reads as "domain-shift" or "generalization" evaluation — including the DCASE anomalous-sound-detection domain-shift challenge itself — is actually **within-machine-type shift** (same rig, same recording setup, different operating condition or machine ID), not transfer across independently recorded testbeds ([DCASE 2021 results](https://dcase.community/challenge2021/task-unsupervised-detection-of-anomalous-sounds-results)). No paper was found that pretrains self-supervised representations on synthetic/simulated acoustic or vibration data and tests transfer to real recorded data — the exact train/test structure AcousticLeakNet already uses — making this a genuine, unaddressed gap rather than solved territory to import from. The field's revealed preference where fine-tuning does occur is parameter-efficient adaptation (LoRA/adapters) rather than full fine-tuning, explicitly because full fine-tuning on small industrial datasets overfits ([arXiv:2508.12230](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.12230)) — this at least suggests that if AcousticLeakNet pursues self-supervised pretraining on its large synthetic corpus followed by light adaptation on small real recordings, that path is more literature-consistent than either full fine-tuning on tiny real data or expecting a frozen off-the-shelf audio foundation model to transfer unmodified.

## Analogous domains show large, consistent transfer collapse without target labels

Four independent analogous-domain literatures converge on the same shape of result, which is the strongest and most load-bearing finding of this whole research pass. In bearing fault diagnosis, a Siamese few-shot method trained only on CWRU and evaluated **with no adaptation** on the independently collected Paderborn dataset (different institution, different sampling rate, naturally vs. artificially seeded faults) achieved just **~54% accuracy**, rising to 96.53% only after adding five labeled target examples per class ([PMC9497688](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9497688/)); a three-rig CWRU/IMS/JNU adversarial-adaptation study found plain DANN with one labeled target sample per class ranged 63.8–80.5% accuracy, improving to 81–99.95% only with a fuller semi-supervised adaptation pipeline ([PMC7374333](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7374333/)). In structural health monitoring, every cross-structure transfer paper found frames the problem explicitly as hard and unsolved without adaptation machinery — one paper's title literally flags zero-shot cross-structure transfer as a distinct, harder special case implying the non-zero-shot norm already requires help ([arXiv:2212.04002](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.04002); [guided-wave cross-structure DA](https://doi.org/10.1177/14759217211010709)). In pipeline leak detection specifically — the most directly relevant analogy found — two 2025–2026 *Water Research* papers test real cross-site transfer: one reports that a global model trained across multiple regions generalizes only **~2 percentage points better** than a model trained on a single region, and that even fine-tuning (reaching 96.2–96.8% accuracy cross-material/cross-diameter) only cuts required target-labeled data by about half, not to zero ([ScienceDirect S0043135425011790](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0043135425011790)); a second reports a Hong Kong-to-Dalian cross-region domain-adaptation framework gaining **>10 percentage points** over a naive baseline, though whether this uses acoustic or pressure/SCADA sensing could not be confirmed ([PubMed 41401765](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41401765/)). No gas-pipeline acoustic-emission paper was found testing cross-rig transfer at all — that literature remains entirely single-testbed.

The most dramatic and directly comparable result comes from underwater acoustics: a tiny CNN trained on one curated maritime-sound archive (USS8, 96.35% in-domain accuracy on an 8-class ship/whale/sonar problem) collapsed to **15.31% accuracy and 5.91% ship-detection rate** when evaluated zero-shot on an independently collected Spanish-port recording dataset (ShipsEar) — an 80-plus-point collapse — and recovered only to ~52% overall accuracy even after adding both a margin-enhanced loss and feature-statistics alignment at inference time, still roughly 45 points below in-domain ([arXiv:2606.28988v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.28988v2)). This is architecturally the closest analogue in the entire review to AcousticLeakNet's own problem: a passive-acoustic, few-class classification task, trained on one independently collected corpus, tested zero-shot on another. Seismic phase-picking looks more encouraging on the surface (models trained on the ~1-million-waveform, 2,613-station STEAD dataset generalize to unseen stations), but this reflects generalization *within* an already-massive, pre-pooled multi-network training set rather than transfer from one small independent dataset to another; the one genuinely independent cross-sensor-type case (land seismometers to ocean-bottom seismometers, OBSTransformer) still required auto-labeled target data and fine-tuning, improving long-range recall by 68–76% over the unadapted baseline, with no clean in-domain OBS ceiling reported to show how much gap remains ([arXiv:2306.04753](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.04753)).

## Conclusion

Three findings should directly reshape AcousticLeakNet's generalization strategy. First, the dataset search confirms there is no shortcut: no new public, multi-material, both-class real water-leak dataset exists to simply add as a fifth source domain, so any near-term progress on "real-world generalization" has to come from better use of the four (or five, pending the Dongguan duplicate check) sources already in hand, not from acquiring a cleaner new one — and the AI Hub Korea corpus and the Dongguan Zenodo release are worth a follow-up access check even though neither solves the dataset-scarcity problem outright. Second, the field-wide pattern across bearing diagnostics, SHM, pipeline leak detection, underwater acoustics, and seismology is unambiguous enough to state as a working law for this project: zero-label cross-rig transfer fails by tens of percentage points everywhere it has been honestly measured, and every published fix assumes some amount of labeled or auto-labeled target access. This means AcousticLeakNet's own anticipated finding — that its synthetic-trained model will not generalize cleanly to unseen real pipes — is not evidence of a flawed pipeline but a direct replication of the field's own best-documented result, and is honestly reportable as such rather than as a shortcoming to engineer away. Third, the single-class-domain confound in the project's real sources is a genuinely novel methodological problem not addressed anywhere in the DG/DA literature surveyed; rather than applying DANN/CORAL/SHOT/Tent off the shelf, the defensible path is to use the balanced synthetic domain for all class-conditional learning and treat the single-class real sites purely as marginal-statistics adaptation targets (CORAL/MMD/AdaBN-style), while explicitly avoiding pseudo-labeling or entropy-minimization methods on single-class batches given their documented risk of confidently reinforcing the wrong class with no counter-signal present to correct it.
